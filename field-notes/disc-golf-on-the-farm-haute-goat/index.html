Disc Golf on the Farm: Telling the Story of Haute Goat

Primary keyword: Haute Goat disc golf · three bracketed items need Jared to confirm before publishing

A championship course on a working farm an hour from Toronto. Here is how we told its story on film, and what it shows about making a place look good and feel like somewhere you want to be.

Some places are easy to photograph and hard to capture. Haute Goat is one of them. It is a working farm just north of Port Hope, Ontario, with goats, a café, and one of the best regarded disc golf courses in the country, good enough to host the Canadian Disc Golf Championship. The challenge with a place like that is not finding something to point a camera at. It is finding the one story that ties all of it together.

The place

Haute Goat is not a sports venue that happens to sit on a farm. It is a farm that built something rare: 18 holes set across rolling hills, a mix of open and wooded play, a 4.7 rating on UDisc, and a championship pedigree. There is a café on site and animals in the field. A visitor remembers the feeling of the place as much as the round they played.

That is the thing most footage of a course misses. You can film 18 good holes and still say nothing about why someone should drive an hour to get there.

The story we chose to tell

We did not set out to film a course. We set out to film a place worth visiting, with the disc golf as the way in.

To do that well, you need a person, not just a landscape. Our preview is built around a player who knows the course, walking it with us. A familiar voice does two things at once. It shows you how the holes actually play, and it gives the viewer someone to follow. The land becomes a story because a person is moving through it with intention.

That is the same approach we bring to a business that is not a farm. Find the person who can carry the story, ask them the right questions, and let the place reveal itself around them.

The craft

A few choices did the quiet work here:

Let the topography lead

Rolling hills are a gift on camera. We let the elevation and the sightlines shape the frames so the course feels like terrain you move through, not a flat map. The land does a lot of the storytelling if you let it.

Calm over hype

It would be easy to cut a course preview like a highlight reel. We kept it calm. A steady pace lets the viewer take in the place and trust what they are seeing, which is closer to how it actually feels to be there.

A real voice, guided

The on camera feature works because the person in it was guided, not left to perform. The right question at the right moment is what turns a knowledgeable player into a natural storyteller. That guidance is the part you do not see and the part that makes all the difference.

What this shows for your project

Haute Goat is a destination. Your business has the same problem in a different shape: there is something true and worth seeing about it, and most video would walk right past it.

The method does not change. We find the story, find the person who can tell it, guide them so they look good and sound good, and build the craft around the moment instead of on top of it. Whether it is a farm, a storefront, or a founder at a desk, the job is the same: make the place and the people feel like somewhere worth your attention.

FAQ

Where is Haute Goat and its disc golf course?

Haute Goat is a working farm just north of Port Hope, Ontario, under an hour east of Toronto. Its 18 hole course is rated 4.7 on UDisc and has hosted championship level play.

Do you only film disc golf and sports?

No. The disc golf preview is one example of a larger approach: telling the story of a place and the people in it. We bring the same method to local businesses, brands, and destinations across Central Ontario and the GTA.

What makes a place "look good" on film?

A clear story and a real person to carry it. Good light and good gear help, but a place feels worth visiting when the footage has a point of view and someone to follow through it.

How do you get a natural performance from someone on camera?

We guide them. The person on screen is not handed a script and left to perform. We ask the right questions and direct calmly in the moment, so what you see is them at their most natural.

Can you tell the story of my business this way?

Yes. If there is something true and worth seeing about what you do, there is a story in it. That is the work.
