Set the Scene

The story is set before the camera rolls.

Filmmakers call it mise-en-scène, French for setting the scene: everything in the frame, arranged on purpose. The space, the light, the props, where a person stands and how they move. Jared learned it on professional film and television sets, and it is the part of the work most video skips.

Get the scene right and the story is already being told, before a word is spoken. It is also what puts people at ease. Give someone a real setting and something to do, and the camera disappears. Great work is not about the gear. It is about the eye that arranges what the gear points at.

Jared Groenewegen
Founder & Director

Jared has been chasing the story behind the moment since he picked up his first camera in high school. In 2016 he moved into film and television in Toronto, where years on professional sets, working alongside directors, producers, and cinematographers, taught him the craft that separates a real film from clean footage: framing, lighting, blocking, and keeping every frame in service of the story. His credits include The Man from Toronto, Mayor of Kingstown, and Rabbit Hole, and he writes and directs his own short films, including The Coffeepot and Shipwreck. What that means for you is simple. He is the calm presence who puts you at ease, so the camera catches the real, unforced version of you, and his attention to detail keeps the story focused, so your video looks professional and actually says something.
